6.1 PROVENANCE & ACTION SAMPLING AUDIT: WHY ARE random_masked NUMBERS IDENTICAL ON 81 VS 209 ACTIONS?
================================================================================
- Question: In results/m7b_partA.csv (pre-Stage-2 simulator, 81 actions) and results/m7b_partB.csv (Stage-2
  simulator, 209 actions), random_masked produces bit-identical destruction (8.69%), stars (0.03), and 3-star
  rate (0.00%) across all 16 held-out pairs. How does random_masked sample actions, and does the mathematical
  explanation for this identical behavior hold?
- Action Sampling Mechanism:
  * At each step, ActionMaskCoCEnv queries mask = env.action_masks(), extracts valid = np.where(mask)[0], and
    draws act = int(rng.choice(valid)) using a seeded NumPy generator (rng = np.random.default_rng(seed=ep)).
  * The Discrete action space is indexed as: action 0 = wait; actions 1 .. (n_troops * num_sectors) represent
    deploying troop k in perimeter edge sector s (with 16 sectors per troop).
- Action Space Expansion and Masking Invariance:
  * In the Stage 2 expansion, 8 new TH8 troop types (wizard, valkyrie, hog rider, golem, golemite, dragon, balloon,
    minion) were strictly appended to data/troops.json after the 5 original ground troops (giant, barbarian,
    archer, wall breaker, goblin). The original 5 troops retained troop indices k = 0..4 (actions 1..80).
  * For the 4 original ground armies evaluated in Part A and Part B (giant_barch, barch, goblin_giant, mixed_wb),
    the counts of all 8 newly added troops in env.army_left[k] are identically 0 throughout the episode.
  * In ActionMaskCoCEnv.action_masks(), the masking loop iterates through troop keys: if env.army_left[k] <= 0,
    the troop is skipped and its action slice remains False. Consequently, actions 81 through 208 are
    unconditionally masked out (False) at every step in every episode of the 4 original ground armies.
  * Therefore, the array of valid actions returned by np.where(mask)[0] is strictly identical to the array of valid
    actions returned under the 81-action space (verified in simulation: max valid action index is 48, well under 81).
  * Because rng.choice(valid) is seeded deterministically with ep, NumPy's PRNG consumes the exact same random
    sequence on an identical candidate list, selecting the exact same integer actions.
  * In step(act), action act < 81 decodes to the exact same (troop_kind, sector), triggering identical spawns.
- Verdict: The mathematical explanation holds completely. The identity of random_masked results between Part A
  and Part B is an architectural guarantee of how troops were appended and how action_masks() restricts actions.

7. LIMITATIONS AND THREATS TO VALIDITY
================================================================================
1. Limited Held-Out Base Evaluation Suite (4 bases):
   * Generalization across unseen defensive layouts is assessed using only 4 held-out layouts (base07.json through
     base10.json). While these layouts reflect distinct defensive archetypes (compartments, perimeter rings,
     funnels, and open central courtyards), 4 layouts constitute a small sample. Army-level star splits rest on only
     4 data points per army, resulting in wide bootstrap confidence intervals (e.g. 95% CI [18.4%, 40.9%] for Part B
     destruction). Subtler layout-dependent failure modes cannot be characterized with statistical significance.
2. Narrow Training Distribution (3 bases):
   * Following the removal of the degenerate, wall-free base01.json, Stage 2 RL policies were trained on only 3 base
     layouts (base02.json, base03.json, base04.json). Exposing the policy to only 3 training layouts risks over-fitting
     to specific defensive geometry, wall junctions, and pathing corridors rather than discovering broad tactical
     principles (e.g. general funneling).
3. Part A Model Checkpoint Overwrite:
   * During Part B multi-seed training on 2026-11-13 (18:25–19:05), the three checkpoint files models/ppo_m7b_seed42.zip,
     seed43.zip, and seed44.zip were overwritten in place by the Part B models (trained on the 209-action space). While
     the complete Part A per-episode dataset (results/m7b_partA_episodes.csv) and summary statistics are preserved, the
     exact Part A neural network weights cannot be loaded or re-evaluated directly on the expanded simulator.
4. Triviality of mass_dragons in Simulator:
   * The mass_dragons army achieves a trivial 100% destruction and 3-star clear across all held-out bases for every
     evaluated agent (left_wave, smart_funnel, surround, PPO, and even random_masked at 96.2% destruction and 81% 3-star
     rate). In the current TH8 simulator, Dragons possess massive hitpoints and fly over all walls immune to ground
     cannons, mortars, and bomb towers. Defenses lack Air Sweepers, Seeking Air Mines, and high-level Clan Castle
     defenders to oppose mass air rushes. Including mass_dragons in the 28-pair benchmark artificially inflates aggregate
     scores and masks true policy deficits on ground armies.
